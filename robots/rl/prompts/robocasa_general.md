Robot: one Panda arm on an Omron mobile base with a lifting support and a parallel-jaw gripper. The general frame origin sits at the arm's current mounting centre on the lifting support, not the simulator origin. +x is forward, +y is the robot's left and +z is up, in metres. Moving the base or lift updates this frame and the support-surface height reported in coordinates: counter tasks report table_height_above_base_m; tasks without a calibrated counter report support_surface="floor" and floor_height_above_base_m, with table_height_above_base_m null.

All reported poses and geometry use the current general frame. For a box or mask, world_xyz is the per-axis median in the recorded point cloud's fixed axes, transformed into the current frame. top_z_m, size_xyz_m, axis_yaw and the z_range_m filter use the current axes. The TCP is the measured midpoint of the two finger-pad centres. The backend compensates its offset from the native grip_site. Use the reported TCP directly without adding a wrist or fingertip offset.

orientation_rpy=[roll,pitch,yaw] is the absolute TCP orientation in radians, R=Rz(yaw)@Ry(pitch)@Rx(roll). The TCP axes follow the grip_site: +z is the approach axis and the jaws close along x. [pi,0,yaw] points the approach axis down. Motion has no collision avoidance. Omitted move target parts keep their measured values at the start of that action. opening is the measured normalized jaw gap, 0 closed and 1 fully open; a blocked closing jaw is not proof of a stable grasp.

Cameras: agentview (left overview) and agentview_right (right overview) move with the base and lifting support; wrist moves with the gripper and is offset from the hand origin, so image-centre alignment is not gripper alignment. Trust the actual displayed camera images rather than assuming pixel directions from another camera or pose. In segment and back_project results, step is the image capture step and coordinate_step is the current frame snapshot used to express world_xyz.

move_base controls bounded local-frame planar velocity and counterclockwise yaw rate, then stops. move_lift raises or lowers the arm's mounting support in metres (positive up, negative down). Both hold the installed arm pose and gripper target, and change the frame origin, camera geometry and support-surface height. Call view_env after any move_base or move_lift before planning further arm motions; back-projecting an older frame transforms fixed scene points into the current frame but cannot reflect objects that moved.
